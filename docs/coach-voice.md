# Ryan's Voice — Parent Guide Writing Rules

Source: Ryan's own messages, coaching cues, and interview answers. Updated as he writes more.

## The core rule
Organize his words. Never rewrite them into something that doesn't sound like him.
If a sentence could have come from any coach, it doesn't sound like Ryan.

## How he sounds
- Short, direct sentences. Fragments are fine when they'd come out of his mouth that way.
- Plainspoken. No throat-clearing, no setup paragraphs, no "in today's fast-paced world."
- Specific over general. Names the drill, the moment, the skill. "Time out drill," not "a core exercise."
- Blunt honesty. "That doesn't work." "Here's what I look for."
- Talks like he's on the floor with you, not lecturing from a stage.

## Never do this
- No em-dashes. He asked twice.
- No "Try asking..." or "Try saying..." patterns. He called this out directly.
- No invented parent confusions ("Many parents wonder..."). He doesn't think those confusions are there.
- No claims that don't hold in all circumstances. He said situations differ and claims that ignore that feel disingenuous.
- No generic encouragement or canned contrasts ("you're not X, you're Y").
- No walls of unbroken text. He called the current layout boring and hard to read.

## Nuance is the point
He keeps saying situations are different and get treated differently. When something
depends on the athlete, say so. "It depends on the kid" is more honest than a rule
that pretends otherwise. He'd rather communicate the nuance than smooth it over.

## His anchor phrases (use his, don't invent new ones)
- "Perfection before progression" — the ORDER skills are built.
- "Progress, not perfection" — the athlete's MINDSET.
- "Celebrate the skill. Build the habits that make it last."

## When writing connective tissue between his answers
- Keep his sentence rhythms. If he writes short, don't expand it into a paragraph.
- If he tells a story, keep the story. Don't extract it into a principle.
- When in doubt, use fewer words, not more.
- Check claims before presenting them as fact. He wants that.
